\title{\sysname{}: Per-Chunk Signed Provenance with Constant-Time\\
Traceback and One-Command Source Revocation for RAG Pipelines}

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines ingest untrusted web sources
with no trust boundary at admission, and corpus poisoning is cheap and
effective: published attacks reach roughly \textbf{\PoisonASR\%} attack success
by injecting only \textbf{\PoisonTexts} malicious passages per target question.
Existing defenses filter at query time, run expensive post-hoc traceback driven
by an iterative language-model judge, or attest documents at ingestion with a
simulated signature and no recovery step. We present \sysname{}, an ingestion
gate that writes, per chunk, an Ed25519-signed provenance record (source,
principal, content hash, detector verdicts) natively into the vector store. This
turns poisoning traceback into a single constant-time signature lookup and
enables one-command revocation of a compromised source. On the BEIR \texttt{nq}
corpus with PoisonedRAG-style injection, \sysname{} attains traceback recall
\textbf{\RTrecall{}} versus \textbf{\BLrecall{}} for an iterative baseline while
performing \textbf{\WorkRatio$\times$} less work, revokes a source
\textbf{\MttrRatio$\times$} faster than a manual purge, and signs at
\textbf{\EdLatency~$\mu$s/chunk}. We release \sysname{} as a reproducible
artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Public bodies and enterprises are deploying retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
over document bases (legislation, citizen-service records, technical support)
whose sources come from the open web with no trust boundary at admission. That
boundary is being attacked. \textbf{PoisonedRAG} shows that injecting only
\PoisonTexts{} malicious passages per target question reaches about
\PoisonASR\% attack success against a knowledge base of millions of texts, and
that existing defenses are insufficient~\cite{poisonedrag}. Carlini et al.
demonstrate that poisoning the very web sources RAG ingests is practical at
web scale, corrupting a measurable fraction of LAION-400M/COYO-700M for tens of
dollars and frontrunning crowd-sourced snapshots such as
Wikipedia~\cite{carlini}. The OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications now mandates,
under \emph{Data and Model Poisoning}, that deployments track data origins and
transformations, vet and sandbox unverified sources, and version their
data.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/OWASP/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/blob/main/2_0_vulns/LLM04_DataModelPoisoning.md}{OWASP
Top 10 for LLM Applications, LLM04:2025 Data and Model Poisoning, OWASP, 2025.}}

This raises a sharp operational question: once a source is found to be
compromised, how quickly and how precisely can an operator attribute the
poisoned chunks to their source and purge every chunk from that source, without
re-running an expensive forensic procedure? Current work answers only part of
this. Query-time filters such as GMTP remove poisoned documents at retrieval but
keep no provenance and offer no recovery~\cite{gmtp}. Traceback systems
(RAGForensics, RAGOrigin) attribute poisoned texts post hoc, triggered by a bad
response, by re-retrieving candidates and judging them with an iterative
language-model prompt: accurate but reactive and costly per
incident~\cite{ragforensics,ragorigin}. The closest ingestion-time work,
RAGShield, frames poisoning as a supply-chain problem and attests content at
admission, but it attests at the \emph{document} level, simulates its Ed25519
signature with a symmetric HMAC, and provides neither signature-keyed traceback
nor source revocation~\cite{ragshield}. A 2026 survey confirms the gap: ``current
work in this direction is still sparse, and systematic support for provenance,
rollback, and corpus recovery remains under-developed''~\cite{ragsurvey}.

We close this gap with \sysname{}, an ingestion gate that writes, per chunk, an
Ed25519-signed provenance record (source URI, principal, SHA-256 content hash,
detector verdicts, timestamp) natively into the vector store. Two side indices
turn the record into operational leverage: attributing a suspect chunk becomes a
single constant-time signature lookup rather than an iterative re-retrieval, and
revoking a compromised source becomes one command that enumerates and purges
exactly that source's chunks. On the BEIR \texttt{nq} corpus with
PoisonedRAG-style injection, \sysname{} attributes every suspect chunk
(\textbf{recall \RTrecall{}}) while a conservative iterative baseline reaches
\textbf{\BLrecall{}}, and does so performing \textbf{\WorkRatio$\times$} less
work. The supply-chain framing and the ingestion gate themselves are not novel;
our contribution is the recovery layer that framing implies but that no surveyed
tool builds. Concretely:

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{C1}] \textbf{Per-chunk signed provenance} embedding the chunk's
  own content hash and admitting detector verdicts directly in the vector store,
  using \emph{real} Ed25519 public-key signatures rather than a symmetric
  stand-in (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
\item[\textbf{C2}] \textbf{Traceback as one constant-time signature lookup}, the
  structural alternative to iterative, language-model-judge-driven re-retrieval,
  evaluated for latency and recall (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\item[\textbf{C3}] \textbf{One-command source revocation} that batch-purges
  every chunk of a compromised principal, the recovery layer absent from all
  surveyed tools, evaluated for mean-time-to-remediation (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\item[\textbf{C4}] A \textbf{reproducible open artifact} with deterministic,
  CPU-only experiments and a digest-pinned corpus, plus ready-to-run harnesses
  for the GPU/API-bound evaluation that is pending (Sections~\ref{sec:eval}
  and~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Detection and query-time filtering.} GMTP filters poisoned documents by
masking high-impact tokens and checking their probability under a masked
language model, eliminating over 90\% of poisoned content at
retrieval~\cite{gmtp}; RevPRAG detects poisoned responses at inference time from
LLM activations~\cite{revprag}; RAGPart and RAGMask are lightweight
retrieval-stage defenses that partition or flag retrieved
content~\cite{ragpartmask}. These contribute effective mitigation of the
\emph{effect} of an attack but keep no provenance, perform no attribution, and
offer no recovery; we treat them as a complementary query-time layer, not
competitors, and claim no novelty in detection.

\textbf{Post-hoc traceback.} RAGForensics introduced traceback for RAG,
re-retrieving candidate texts and prompting an LLM to judge which are
poisoned~\cite{ragforensics}; RAGOrigin extends this to black-box responsibility
attribution with an adaptive scope and rank/relevance/influence
scoring~\cite{ragorigin}. Both are valuable forensic tools, but both are
reactive and triggered per bad response, and their iterative judging is costly
at forensic time; neither attests provenance at ingestion nor revokes a source.
\sysname{} is complementary: it pays a small one-time signing cost at ingestion
so that attribution later becomes a constant-time lookup, and adds the missing
revocation step.

\textbf{Ingestion-time attestation.} RAGShield is the closest prior work, and we
credit it: it frames RAG poisoning as a supply-chain problem and attests content
at admission~\cite{ragshield}. Verified against its own text, however, it attests
at the document level, simulates its Ed25519 signature with a symmetric HMAC (its
Table IV), reports only ASR and FPR, and provides no signature-keyed traceback or
source revocation. D-RAG admits content with provenance
and human expert verification on a blockchain, but at document or source
granularity, with heavy machinery and no
one-command purge.\footnote{\href{https://aircconline.com/csit/papers/vol15/csit150417.pdf}{D-RAG:
A Privacy-Preserving Framework for Decentralized RAG using Blockchain, CSIT,
2025.}} The generic supply-chain analogues, namely Sigstore/Cosign signing and
the CycloneDX ML-BOM, sign at file granularity, carry no detector verdicts, and
are not instantiated for the fragments that RAG actually
retrieves.\footnote{\href{https://edu.chainguard.dev/open-source/sigstore/cosign/how-to-sign-an-sbom-with-cosign/}{How
to Sign an SBOM with Cosign, Chainguard Academy, 2024.}} \sysname{} is orthogonal
to all of these: per-chunk granularity, a real public-key signature, and the
recovery layer of traceback plus revocation that the analogy implies, as the
rightmost columns of Table~\ref{tab:related} make explicit.

\section{Design}\label{sec:design}

\sysname{} is an ingestion gate. For each chunk it computes a SHA-256 content
hash, runs a best-effort detector suite, assembles a canonical byte string over
the provenance tuple (chunk id, source URI, principal, content hash, detector
verdicts, timestamp, signer identity, granularity), signs it with the configured
backend, and writes the signed record into the datastore. The flow is:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\raggedright\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}%
\textbf{[1] Gate.} For each chunk: normalize and chunk; run detectors to obtain
verdicts; compute $h=\mathrm{SHA\text{-}256}(\text{chunk})$; sign
$\sigma=\mathrm{Ed25519}\bigl(k_{\mathrm{priv}},\ \text{URI}\,\|\,
\text{principal}\,\|\,h\,\|\,\text{verdicts}\,\|\,t\bigr)$.\par
\textbf{[2] Store.} Write the per-chunk record
$\langle\text{id},\text{URI},\text{principal},h,\text{verdicts},t,\sigma\rangle$
into the vector store; normal RAG queries are unchanged.\par
\textbf{[3] Traceback.} A suspect chunk is resolved by one constant-time
signature/hash lookup.\par
\textbf{[4] Revoke.} \texttt{revoke-source <principal>} batch-purges every chunk
of that principal.}}
\end{center}

\textbf{Why this is sound.} The datastore maintains two side indices. A
content-hash index maps a chunk hash to its chunk id, resolving a suspect chunk
to its signed record in constant time. A principal index maps a principal to its
set of chunk ids, so revoking a source enumerates exactly its chunks without
scanning the corpus. Signatures use real Ed25519, so provenance is
non-repudiable and verifiable by any holder of the public key. The signed
message binds the verdicts and the content hash, so a record cannot be replayed
for different content or different admitting verdicts without invalidating the
signature.

\textbf{Threat model and scope.} The attacker controls or compromises a web
source ingested by the corpus and injects poisoned texts, including the
split-view/frontrunning case where a source mutates after
collection~\cite{poisonedrag,carlini}. The protected asset is the integrity and
\emph{traceability} of the indexed corpus, and the secondary asset is
mean-time-to-remediation after a source is found compromised. The operator is
trusted and holds the private key; ingestion is where provenance is sealed.
\sysname{} does not claim to detect all poisoning at ingestion: detectors are
best-effort and may have false negatives, so the value is forensic and
operational, not perfect detection. Query-time detectors~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask}
are a complementary layer.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Behaviour is configured entirely by environment
variables (prefix \texttt{RAGTRAP\_}); nothing is hardcoded. Every run logs to
the console and to a timestamped file, recording the resolved configuration,
each input with its content hash, every step, and all outputs. A manifest pins
each input by content digest, including the BEIR passage subset by the SHA-256
of its concatenated passage texts (\texttt{\SubsetDigest{}\ldots}), the Hugging
Face revision, and the passage cap. The private key is generated per run and
never written to disk.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Setup.} Experiments run CPU-only and offline. The real corpus is the
BEIR \texttt{nq} passage corpus loaded from its public Hugging Face
mirror,\footnote{\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/BeIR/nq}{BeIR/nq dataset
(Natural Questions passages), Hugging Face.}} capped at \NpassCap{} passages for
this session and pinned by digest (Section~\ref{sec:design}); the cap is stated
and configurable, and the cap-removed full-corpus run is experiment E7
(pending). The \NpassCap{} passages yield \NcleanChunks{} clean chunks at a
512-character window with 64-character overlap. Poisoned chunks are reconstructed
from the published PoisonedRAG black-box template (not optimizer output) and
labelled as such. Wall-clock latencies vary slightly between runs; the
deterministic quantities (work-unit counts, recall, false-purge rate, signature
and record sizes) are stable. We first validated the instrument on a labelled
synthetic corpus of 200 chunks across 5 principals (E0): all signed records
verify, a tampered message is rejected, traceback recall is \RTrecall{}, and
revocation purges exactly the targeted principal's 20 chunks with no collateral.

\textbf{E1: Traceback latency and recall (headline).} We attribute the
\NsuspectsE{} suspect chunks two ways: \sysname{}'s constant-time signature
lookup, and a scripted iterative-attribution baseline that re-retrieves and
scans candidate texts. The baseline is the algorithmic shape of
response-triggered attribution without the language-model judge, a deliberately
conservative lower bound on the published baseline's cost. As
Table~\ref{tab:e1e2} (left) shows, \sysname{} attributes every suspect chunk
(\textbf{recall \RTrecall{}}) while the iterative baseline reaches \BLrecall{},
because near-identical poisoned texts confuse lexical re-retrieval whereas the
signed content hash resolves each suspect unambiguously. \sysname{} performs
\textbf{\WorkRatio$\times$} less work (\RTwork{} constant-time lookups versus
\BLwork{} corpus comparisons), and is about \LatRatio$\times$ faster in
wall-clock on this run (\RTlat~s versus \BLlat~s). The latency advantage is
therefore not bought at the cost of correctness: \sysname{} is simultaneously
faster and strictly more accurate. The work-unit ratio is the run-invariant form
of the headline; the published language-model-judge baseline would be slower
still, adding a model call per candidate.

\textbf{E2: Mean-time-to-remediation.} Treating one principal as compromised, we
remove it two ways: \sysname{}'s \texttt{revoke-source} batch purge versus a
manual loop that scans the whole corpus. Table~\ref{tab:e1e2} (right) shows batch
revocation removes the \RevChunks{} chunks of the source about
\textbf{\MttrRatio$\times$} faster on this run. The advantage is structural:
revocation cost scales with the revoked principal's chunk count while the manual
scan scales with the whole corpus, so the ratio grows with corpus size (the
full-corpus measurement is E7, pending).

\textbf{E3: Per-chunk versus per-document granularity.} On a partially-poisoned
document of \MixClean{} clean and \MixPoison{} poisoned chunks under one
principal, the per-document scheme (sign the parent, propagate trust)
over-purges \PerDocFalse{} of the \PerDocPurged{} chunks it removes
(false-purge rate \textbf{\PerDocFP{}}) because it cannot distinguish clean from
poisoned fragments, whereas \sysname{}'s per-chunk scheme attributes all poisoned
chunks (recall \RTrecall{}) with \textbf{zero} false purge (rate \PerChunkFP{}).
Both configurations are \sysname{}'s own; no external number is used here.

\textbf{E4: Ingestion overhead and storage.} On the real corpus, signing each
chunk with real Ed25519 costs about \textbf{\EdLatency} microseconds, which is
roughly \EdThroughput{} chunks per second on a single thread, and produces a
fixed \EdSig{}-byte signature inside a record of about \EdRecord{} bytes. This is
near \textbf{\EdOverHmac} times the cost of the symmetric HMAC stand-in, which
signs in about \HmacLatency{} microseconds with a \HmacSig{}-byte signature. In
exchange, public-key signing yields non-repudiable provenance that anyone can
verify without the secret key, the very capability that the simulated HMAC of
RAGShield does not provide~\cite{ragshield}. A labelled synthetic sweep over
corpora of 1000, 5000, and 10000 chunks confirms that the per-chunk cost stays
near \EdSweepLat{} microseconds and does not grow with corpus size. The overhead
is small enough for practical ingestion.

\textbf{Pending evaluation.} Three experiments cannot run in this CPU-only,
offline session and are reported as pending with ready-to-run harnesses; no
number is fabricated for them. \textbf{E5} (faithful head-to-head against the
published RAGForensics/RAGOrigin) needs an LLM-judge API key, an embedding
retriever, and a checkout of the official
repository,\footnote{\href{https://github.com/zhangbl6618/RAG-Responsibility-Attribution}{RAG-Responsibility-Attribution
(RAGOrigin code), 2025.}} so E1 reports against the labelled iterative-attribution
lower bound, which the published judge-driven baseline can only exceed in cost.
\textbf{E6} (end-to-end PoisonedRAG attack success and a GMTP query-time layer)
needs a GPU, a generation model, and a masked-language-model reranker; since
\sysname{}'s claims are forensic and not attack-success, this is positioning
evidence only. \textbf{E7} (full-scale corpora) runs the same E1/E4 code path
with the passage cap raised, and needs more RAM, time, and cores. To be reported
when those resources are available (experiments pending: API/GPU/scale).

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Threats to validity.} External validity: results are on the bounded BEIR
\texttt{nq} subset; the digest-pinned, cap-stated design makes the full-corpus
run (E7) a single configuration change, and the structural arguments (constant
work for lookup and revocation) do not depend on scale. Baseline fairness: E1
uses a conservative iterative lower bound, not the published judge; the faithful
head-to-head (E5) is pending and can only widen the gap. Detection: ingestion
detectors are best-effort, so false negatives leave chunks unflagged; the value
is forensic, with query-time filters as a complementary
layer~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask}. Key management: we assume a trusted operator
holding the private key, and key compromise is a residual risk.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Corpus poisoning is cheap and effective, standards now
demand provenance and quarantine, and the closest tools either filter at query
time, attribute post hoc with a costly iterative judge, or attest documents with
a simulated signature and no recovery step. The operational question we opened,
how fast and how precisely can an operator attribute and purge a compromised
source, has a concrete answer: by sealing per-chunk Ed25519 provenance at
ingestion, \sysname{} turns traceback into a constant-time lookup (recall
\RTrecall{} versus \BLrecall{} at \WorkRatio$\times$ less work) and turns
recovery into one command (\MttrRatio$\times$ faster than a manual purge), at an
ingestion cost of \EdLatency~$\mu$s/chunk. This is the recovery layer the
supply-chain analogy implies but that no surveyed tool had built. We release
\sysname{} as a reproducible, double-blind artifact.\footnote{\href{https://anonymous.4open.science}{Anonymized
artifact repository (link provided for double-blind review).}}
