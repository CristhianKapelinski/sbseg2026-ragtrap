\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds a language model on passages
fetched from an external corpus, yet that corpus is ingested from untrusted
sources with no trust boundary at admission. Corpus poisoning is cheap:
published attacks reach about \textbf{\PoisonASR\%} attack success by injecting
\textbf{\PoisonTexts} passages per question. Existing forensics re-retrieve and
score suspects at incident time, paying many model calls per incident and
keeping no provenance for recovery. We present \sysname{}, an ingestion gate
that writes a per-chunk Ed25519-signed provenance record into the vector store,
turning traceback into one constant-time content-hash lookup and enabling
one-command revocation of a compromised source. On the real PoisonedRAG attack
over Natural Questions, attributing the same \textbf{\NsuspectsE{}} suspects as
two forensic baselines run here on identical inputs, \sysname{} runs
\textbf{$\sim$\LatSpeedup$\times$} and \textbf{$\sim$\LatSpeedupRO$\times$}
faster with no model calls, revokes a source up to
\textbf{\FullMttrRatio$\times$} faster than a full-corpus purge, and degrades to
\textbf{\RTrecallDriftThree{}} recall under 30\% post-ingestion drift. We release
\sysname{} as a reproducible artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), the technique of answering a query by
first retrieving relevant passages from an external document corpus and then
conditioning a large language model (LLM) on them, has become the dominant way
organizations deploy LLMs over their own data. Enterprise RAG adoption rose to
\textbf{51\%} in 2024 from 31\% a year
earlier,\footnote{\href{https://menlovc.com/2024-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/}{Menlo
Ventures, ``2024: The State of Generative AI in the Enterprise'', 2024.}} and
around \textbf{70\%} of organizations using generative AI now ground their
models with retrieval systems and vector databases rather than off-the-shelf
models alone, a category that grew 377\% year over
year.\footnote{\href{https://www.databricks.com/blog/state-ai-enterprise-adoption-growth-trends}{Databricks,
``State of AI: Enterprise Adoption \& Growth Trends'', 2024.}} The RAG market,
estimated at \textbf{USD 1.2 billion} in 2024, is projected to grow at a
\textbf{49.1\%} compound annual rate through
2030.\footnote{\href{https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-market-report}{Grand
View Research, ``Retrieval Augmented Generation Market Size Report, 2030''.}}
The pattern is consequential precisely because it carries high-stakes content:
government agencies adopt RAG to answer citizens from official documents while
keeping regulated data in place and citing the source of each
answer,\footnote{\href{https://fedtechmagazine.com/article/2025/05/how-rag-boosts-agency-accuracy-security-perfcon}{A.~Slagg,
``How RAG Boosts Agency Accuracy and Security'', FedTech, 2025.}}$^{,}$\footnote{\href{https://statetechmagazine.com/article/2025/02/what-is-rag-perfcon}{StateTech,
``What Is Retrieval Augmented Generation, and How Are State and Local Agencies
Using It?'', 2025.}} so the integrity and traceability of the corpus is a
first-class requirement.

That corpus is the attack surface. Because passages are ingested from the open
web and shared document stores with no trust boundary at admission, an adversary
who controls even a few sources can poison the answers. PoisonedRAG shows that
injecting only \PoisonTexts{} malicious passages per target question into a
knowledge base of millions of texts reaches about \PoisonASR\% attack success,
steering the model to an attacker-chosen answer~\cite{poisonedrag}. Carlini
et~al.\ show this is practical at web scale: an attacker could poison a fraction
of LAION-400M-class datasets for about sixty dollars, and can frontrun the
periodic snapshots of crowd-sourced sources such as Wikipedia that RAG corpora
are built from~\cite{carlini}. Standards now treat this as a supply-chain risk:
the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications lists \emph{Data and Model Poisoning}
(LLM04:2025) and mandates that deployments track data origins and
transformations, vet and sandbox unverified sources, and version their
data.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/OWASP/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/blob/main/2_0_vulns/LLM04_DataModelPoisoning.md}{OWASP
Top 10 for LLM Applications, LLM04:2025 Data and Model Poisoning, 2025.}}

This raises a sharp operational question that existing work answers only in
part: once a source is found compromised, how quickly and how precisely can an
operator attribute the poisoned chunks to their source and purge every chunk
from that source? Query-time filters such as GMTP (a gradient-based masked-token
probability detector) remove poisoned documents at retrieval but keep no
provenance and offer no recovery~\cite{gmtp}. Forensic attributors, RAGForensics
and RAGOrigin, attribute poisoned texts post hoc: triggered by a bad answer,
they re-retrieve candidate passages and score each one, RAGForensics by
prompting an LLM judge per candidate~\cite{ragforensics} and RAGOrigin by
scoring each candidate with a proxy LLM and clustering~\cite{ragorigin}, an
approach that is accurate but reactive and pays many model invocations per
incident. None of these tools keeps the provenance needed to attribute in
constant time or to revoke a source in one step. A 2026 survey confirms the gap:
systematic support for provenance, rollback, and corpus recovery remains
under-developed~\cite{ragsurvey}.

We close this gap with \sysname{}, an ingestion gate that writes, per chunk, an
Ed25519-signed provenance record (source URI, principal, SHA-256 content hash,
detector verdicts, timestamp) into the vector store. Two side indices turn the
record into operational leverage: attributing a suspect chunk becomes a single
constant-time content-hash lookup instead of an iterative re-retrieval, and
revoking a compromised source becomes one command that enumerates and purges
exactly that source's chunks. On the real PoisonedRAG attack over Natural
Questions~\cite{naturalquestions}, with the \NsuspectsE{} top-ranked suspects
surfaced by the released \texttt{e5} dense retriever~\cite{e5}, \sysname{}'s
single lookup attributes each suspect with no model invocation, about
\textbf{\LatSpeedup$\times$} faster than the RAGForensics LLM-judge baseline and
\textbf{\LatSpeedupRO$\times$} faster than the RAGOrigin proxy-scoring baseline,
both run here on identical inputs, and it revokes a compromised source in one
command. The supply-chain framing and the ingestion gate are not themselves
novel; the contribution is the recovery layer that framing implies but that no
surveyed tool builds.

This paper makes four contributions.
\textbf{(C1)} A per-chunk signed-provenance gate (Section~\ref{sec:design}) that
embeds the chunk's own content hash and admitting detector verdicts in the
vector store with Ed25519 public-key signatures.
\textbf{(C2)} Traceback reduced to one constant-time content-hash lookup,
measured against two forensic baselines on identical third-party inputs (E2,
Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\textbf{(C3)} One-command source revocation that batch-purges every chunk of a
compromised principal, evaluated for mean-time-to-remediation at corpus scale
(E3, Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\textbf{(C4)} A reproducible open artifact (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) with a
digest-pinned real corpus, third-party attack and baseline code, and an
end-to-end harness.

\section{Related Work}

We position \sysname{} against four lines of work, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:related}. \textbf{Detection and query-time filtering.} GMTP
filters poisoned documents by masking high-impact tokens and checking their
probability under a masked language model, eliminating over 90\% of poisoned
content at retrieval~\cite{gmtp}; RevPRAG detects poisoned responses at inference
time from LLM activations~\cite{revprag}; RAGPart and RAGMask are retrieval-stage
defenses that partition or flag retrieved content~\cite{ragpartmask}. These
mitigate the \emph{effect} of an attack but keep no provenance, perform no
attribution, and offer no recovery; they are a complementary query-time layer,
and on detector accuracy a filter like GMTP is the stronger tool. \sysname{}
targets the operational axis those tools do not address and claims no novelty in
detection. RevPRAG and GMTP act at retrieval/inference time, not at forensic
time over a fixed suspect list, so they are not run as attributors here.

\textbf{Forensic attribution.} RAGForensics introduced traceback for RAG,
re-retrieving candidate texts and prompting an LLM to judge which are
poisoned~\cite{ragforensics}; RAGOrigin extends this to black-box responsibility
attribution with an adaptive scope and rank/relevance/influence
scoring~\cite{ragorigin}. Both report strong attribution accuracy, but both are
reactive, triggered per bad response, and their per-candidate scoring is costly
at forensic time; neither attests provenance at ingestion nor revokes a source.
\sysname{} is complementary: it pays a small one-time signing cost at ingestion
so that attribution later becomes a constant-time lookup, and adds the missing
revocation step. We run both as baselines on identical suspects in
Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

\textbf{Ingestion-time attestation.} Generic supply-chain signing analogues sign
at file granularity, carry no detector verdicts, and are not instantiated for the
fragments that RAG retrieves: Sigstore/Cosign signs whole
artifacts,\footnote{\href{https://edu.chainguard.dev/open-source/sigstore/cosign/how-to-sign-an-sbom-with-cosign/}{How
to Sign an SBOM with Cosign, Chainguard Academy, 2024.}} as does the CycloneDX
ML-BOM. D-RAG admits content
with provenance and human expert verification on a blockchain, but at document
or source granularity, with heavy machinery and no one-command
purge.\footnote{\href{https://aircconline.com/csit/papers/vol15/csit150417.pdf}{D-RAG:
A Privacy-Preserving Framework for Decentralized RAG using Blockchain, CSIT,
2025.}} \sysname{} is orthogonal: per-chunk granularity, a real public-key
signature, and the recovery layer of traceback plus revocation that the analogy
implies, as the rightmost columns of Table~\ref{tab:related} make explicit.
\textbf{Numerical-claim verification.} Orthogonal to provenance, RAGShield
verifies extracted dollar amounts and percentages in government RAG text against
a cross-source registry, reporting 0.0\% attack success on its numerical
manipulation benchmark~\cite{ragshield}; it does not attest provenance, attribute
sources, or revoke, and is a complementary content-level check.

\section{Design}\label{sec:design}

\sysname{} is an ingestion gate (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). For each chunk it
computes a SHA-256 content hash, runs a best-effort detector suite, assembles a
canonical byte string over the provenance tuple (chunk id, source URI,
principal, content hash, detector verdicts, timestamp, signer identity,
granularity), signs it with Ed25519, and writes the signed record into the
datastore. The datastore maintains two side indices. A content-hash index maps a
chunk hash to its signed record, so an exact content-hash match resolves a
suspect chunk to the principal recorded for it at ingestion in constant time:
this exact-hash match is the traceback operation. A principal index maps a
principal to its set of chunk ids, so revoking a source enumerates exactly its
chunks without scanning the corpus. Because the signed message binds the
verdicts and the content hash, a record cannot be replayed for different content
or different admitting verdicts without invalidating the signature, and any
holder of the public key can verify provenance.

\textbf{Threat model and scope.} The attacker controls or compromises a web
source ingested by the corpus and injects poisoned texts, including the
split-view/frontrunning case where a source mutates after
collection~\cite{poisonedrag,carlini}. The protected asset is the integrity and
\emph{traceability} of the indexed corpus; the secondary asset is
mean-time-to-remediation after a source is found compromised. The operator is
trusted and holds the private key; ingestion is where provenance is sealed.
Detection at ingestion is best-effort and may have false negatives, so
\sysname{}'s value is forensic and operational; query-time
detectors~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask} are a complementary layer.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Behaviour is configured entirely by environment
variables (prefix \texttt{RAGTRAP\_}); nothing is hardcoded. Every run logs the
resolved configuration, each input with its content hash, every step, and all
outputs to a timestamped file. A manifest pins each third-party input by SHA-256
(Table~\ref{tab:data}) alongside model identities. The private key is generated
per run and never written to disk.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

We run four experiments on the datasets of Table~\ref{tab:data}, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:exps}. \textbf{E1} validates the instrument on a labelled corpus.
\textbf{E2} compares forensic-time attribution on identical real suspects
against two baselines (delivering C2), and measures recall under post-ingestion
drift. \textbf{E3} measures revocation mean-time-to-remediation and per-chunk
ingestion overhead at corpus scale (delivering C1 and C3). \textbf{E4} confirms
the suspects are genuinely dangerous via attack success. The attack, retrieval,
ground-truth labels, and baselines are all third-party artifacts that \sysname{}
did not author, so the suspect set used for attribution is independent of the
mechanism under test.

\textbf{E1: Instrument validation.} On a labelled corpus of \EZeroChunks{}
chunks, all signed records verify, a tampered message is rejected, and revocation
purges exactly the targeted principal's \EZeroPurged{} chunks with no collateral
removal. This confirms the gate, signature verification, and the revocation
index behave as specified before any comparison.

\textbf{E2: Forensic-time attribution on the real attack.} The clean substrate
is the BEIR~\cite{beir} Natural Questions corpus~\cite{naturalquestions}; the
attack is PoisonedRAG's released adversarial passages~\cite{poisonedrag}; the
suspects and labels come from the released RAGOrigin attack-feedback file (the
contexts surfaced by the \texttt{e5} dense retriever~\cite{e5} for each of
\Nquestions{} target questions, with third-party poisoned/clean labels). For
each question we ingest its retrieved contexts through the \sysname{} gate (real
Ed25519 per chunk). At forensic time the suspects are the top-\TopK{} retrieved
contexts per question (\NsuspectsE{} suspects: \NpoisonE{} poisoned, \NcleanE{}
clean), exactly what an attacked pipeline feeds the LLM. Three attributors run on
the \emph{identical} \NsuspectsE{} suspects, all executed here: \sysname{}'s
constant-time content-hash lookup; the published RAGForensics LLM-judge loop run
verbatim over a local \BLjudge{} model (one model call per context); and the
published RAGOrigin responsibility scoring run over a local \ROproxy{} proxy
(answer-loss, question-loss, and retrieval score, z-normalized and
K-means-thresholded, two proxy calls per context).

\textbf{E2-drift: recall under post-ingestion mutation.} \sysname{} resolves a
suspect only if its bytes were admitted through the gate, so a poisoned chunk
whose retrieved bytes drift after ingestion (a paraphrase, the split-view

is exact; the missed chunks are false negatives. Recovering drifted variants
requires near-duplicate or semantic matching and is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\textbf{E3: Revocation MTTR and ingestion cost at scale.} Treating one
PoisonedRAG attacker principal as compromised, we remove it two ways on the real
corpus: \sysname{}'s \texttt{revoke-source} batch purge versus a manual loop that
scans the whole corpus. \sysname{} reads the principal's \RevChunks{} chunk ids
from the index and purges them in \textbf{\FullRevMttr~$\mu$s} on the full
\FullChunks{}-chunk corpus, while the manual scan takes \FullManMttr~ms, a
\textbf{\FullMttrRatio$\times$} advantage (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). The advantage
is structural: revocation cost scales with the revoked principal's chunk count
while the manual scan scales with the whole corpus, so the ratio grows from
\SmallMttrRatio$\times$ at \SmallPassages{} passages to \FullMttrRatio$\times$ at
the full corpus. Per-chunk Ed25519 signing costs about \textbf{\EdLatency~$\mu$s}
($\sim$\EdThroughput{} chunks/s, single-threaded), producing a fixed
\EdSig{}-byte signature in an \EdRecord{}-byte record. This is about
\textbf{\EdOverHmac$\times$} the cost of a symmetric HMAC stand-in
(\HmacLatency~$\mu$s, \HmacSig{}-byte tag); in exchange, public-key signing
yields non-repudiable provenance verifiable without the secret key. We also
contrast per-chunk against per-document granularity: on \EThreeDocs{} real
partially-poisoned documents (real NQ passages with real PoisonedRAG passages
injected under one principal), the per-document scheme over-purges clean
fragments at a false-purge rate of \textbf{\PerDocFP{}} (95\%~CI
[\PerDocFPLo{},\,\PerDocFPHi{}], \PerDocFalse{} of \PerDocPurged{} removed chunks

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Threats to validity.} E2's suspects and labels come from one released
attack-feedback set over NQ; the corpus-scale results (E3) use the full
\FullPassages{}-passage corpus, and the structural arguments (constant work for
lookup and revocation) do not depend on scale. The two baselines are the
published RAGForensics and RAGOrigin procedures run over local open models rather
than the hosted models the original papers used; we report our own measured
recall, precision, latency, and model-call count on identical inputs.
\sysname{}'s exact-hash attribution misses post-ingestion byte drift (E2-drift);
near-duplicate and semantic matching of suspects, adaptive and multi-attacker
regimes, and other corpora (HotpotQA, MS-MARCO, whose released attack data we
also obtained) are future work. Detection itself is out of scope and
best-effort, with query-time filters as the complementary
layer~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask}. We assume a trusted operator holding the private
key; key compromise is a residual risk.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Corpus poisoning is cheap and effective, standards demand
provenance and quarantine, and the closest tools either filter at query time or
attribute post hoc with many model calls and no recovery step. The operational
question we opened, how fast and how cheaply an operator can attribute and purge
a compromised source, has a concrete answer: by sealing per-chunk Ed25519
provenance at ingestion, \sysname{} makes traceback a single constant-time
lookup, about \LatSpeedup$\times$ and \LatSpeedupRO$\times$ faster than two
forensic baselines on identical suspects with no model calls, degrading to
\RTrecallDriftThree{} recall under 30\% post-ingestion drift, and it turns
recovery into one command whose advantage over a full-corpus purge grows to
\FullMttrRatio$\times$ at the full \FullPassages{}-passage corpus, at an
ingestion cost of \EdLatency~$\mu$s/chunk. This is the recovery layer the
supply-chain analogy implies but that no surveyed tool had built. We release
\sysname{} as a reproducible, double-blind
artifact.\footnote{\href{https://anonymous.4open.science}{Anonymized artifact
repository (link provided for double-blind review).}}
