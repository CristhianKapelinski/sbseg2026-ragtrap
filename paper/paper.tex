\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds a language model on an external
corpus ingested from untrusted sources with no admission trust boundary, and
corpus poisoning is cheap: published attacks reach about \textbf{\PoisonASR\%}
attack success by injecting \textbf{\PoisonTexts} passages per question. Once a
detector or analyst flags a compromised source, the open problem is recovery:
purging that source cleanly without scanning the corpus or discarding benign
content. \sysname{} is a recovery and inventory layer that records per-chunk
signed provenance at ingestion so revocation purges exactly the compromised
principal's chunks at a false-purge rate of \textbf{\PerChunkFP{}}, against
\textbf{\PerDocFP{}} for document-level purging, and traceback becomes one
content-hash lookup. On the real PoisonedRAG attack over Natural Questions the
lookup matches two forensic baselines with no model calls. Recovery rests on
exact content-hash match, so recall falls to \textbf{\RTrecallDriftThree{}}
under 30\% post-ingestion drift; \sysname{} neither detects nor prevents
poisoning. We release a reproducible artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), the technique of answering a query by
first retrieving relevant passages from an external document corpus and then
conditioning a large language model (LLM) on them, has become the dominant way
organizations deploy LLMs over their own data. Enterprise RAG adoption rose to
\textbf{51\%} in 2024 from 31\% a year
earlier,\footnote{\href{https://menlovc.com/2024-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/}{Menlo
Ventures, ``2024: The State of Generative AI in the Enterprise'', 2024.}} and
around \textbf{70\%} of organizations using generative AI now ground their
models with retrieval systems and vector databases rather than off-the-shelf
models alone, a category that grew 377\% year over
year.\footnote{\href{https://www.databricks.com/blog/state-ai-enterprise-adoption-growth-trends}{Databricks,
``State of AI: Enterprise Adoption \& Growth Trends'', 2024.}} The RAG market,
estimated at \textbf{USD 1.2 billion} in 2024, is projected to grow at a
\textbf{49.1\%} compound annual rate through
2030.\footnote{\href{https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-market-report}{Grand
View Research, ``Retrieval Augmented Generation Market Size Report, 2030''.}}
The pattern is consequential precisely because it carries high-stakes content:
government agencies adopt RAG to answer citizens from official documents while
keeping regulated data in place and citing the source of each
answer,\footnote{\href{https://fedtechmagazine.com/article/2025/05/how-rag-boosts-agency-accuracy-security-perfcon}{A.~Slagg,
``How RAG Boosts Agency Accuracy and Security'', FedTech, 2025.}}$^{,}$\footnote{\href{https://statetechmagazine.com/article/2025/02/what-is-rag-perfcon}{StateTech,
``What Is Retrieval Augmented Generation, and How Are State and Local Agencies
Using It?'', 2025.}} so the integrity and traceability of the corpus is a
first-class requirement.

That corpus is the attack surface. Because passages are ingested from the open
web and shared document stores with no trust boundary at admission, an adversary
who controls even a few sources can poison the answers. PoisonedRAG shows that
injecting only \PoisonTexts{} malicious passages per target question into a
knowledge base of millions of texts reaches about \PoisonASR\% attack success,
steering the model to an attacker-chosen answer~\cite{poisonedrag}. Carlini
et~al.\ show this is practical at web scale: an attacker could poison a fraction
of LAION-400M-class datasets for about sixty dollars, and can frontrun the
periodic snapshots of crowd-sourced sources such as Wikipedia that RAG corpora
are built from~\cite{carlini}. Standards now treat this as a supply-chain risk:
the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications lists \emph{Data and Model Poisoning}
(LLM04:2025) and mandates that deployments track data origins and
transformations, vet and sandbox unverified sources, and version their
data.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/OWASP/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/blob/main/2_0_vulns/LLM04_DataModelPoisoning.md}{OWASP
Top 10 for LLM Applications, LLM04:2025 Data and Model Poisoning, 2025.}}

This raises a sharp operational question that existing work answers only in
part: once a source is found compromised, how quickly and how precisely can an
operator attribute the poisoned chunks to their source and purge every chunk
from that source? Query-time filters such as GMTP (a gradient-based masked-token
probability detector) remove poisoned documents at retrieval but keep no
provenance and offer no recovery~\cite{gmtp}. Forensic attributors, RAGForensics
and RAGOrigin, descend from poisoning traceback in neural-network training, which
traces a successful attack back to the responsible training samples by iterative
clustering and unlearning~\cite{poison-forensics}; they attribute poisoned texts
post hoc: triggered by a bad answer, they re-retrieve candidate passages and
evaluate each one, RAGForensics by prompting an LLM judge to label each candidate
poisoned or benign~\cite{ragforensics} and RAGOrigin by scoring each candidate
with a proxy LLM and clustering~\cite{ragorigin}, an approach that is accurate but
reactive and pays many model invocations per incident. None of these tools keeps the
provenance needed to attribute in constant time or to revoke a source in one
step. A 2026 survey confirms the gap:
knowledge-base integrity and provenance defenses remain sparse, and
knowledge-base governance and rollback is identified as a thin, under-developed
area~\cite{ragsurvey}.

We close this gap with \sysname{}, a recovery and inventory layer that builds on
an ingestion gate writing, per chunk, an Ed25519-signed provenance record
(source URI, principal, SHA-256 content hash, detector verdicts, timestamp) into
the vector store. Per-chunk signed provenance is itself anticipated by prior and
concurrent work: cryptographic provenance protocols sign individual RAG
embeddings to their source-content hash~\cite{VectorPin}, bind answers to signed
source evidence with portable receipts~\cite{leray2025policy}, and commit
chunk-localised fingerprints in issuer-signed Merkle trees in adjacent
domains~\cite{MerkleSpeech}. \sysname{}'s contribution is the engineered recovery
layer that the same record enables and that none of these systems provides
together: two side indices turn the record into operational leverage. A principal
index makes revoking a compromised source one command that enumerates and purges
exactly that source's chunks, surgically: on real partially-poisoned documents
this localises the poisoned chunks at a false-purge rate of
\textbf{\PerChunkFP{}}, against \textbf{\PerDocFP{}} when purging is done at
document granularity. A content-hash index makes attributing a suspect chunk a
single constant-time lookup instead of an iterative re-retrieval. On the real
PoisonedRAG attack over Natural Questions~\cite{naturalquestions}, with the
\NsuspectsE{} top-ranked suspects surfaced by the released \texttt{e5} dense
retriever~\cite{e5}, the lookup attributes each suspect with no model invocation,
matching two forensic baselines run here on identical inputs while costing no
model calls. \sysname{} is a forensic and operational recovery layer: it depends
on an external detector or analyst to flag a compromised source and does not
itself detect or prevent poisoning.

This paper makes three contributions.
\textbf{(C1)} Surgical one-command source revocation: a principal-indexed
batch purge that removes exactly a compromised principal's chunks, evaluated
against document-level purging for false-purge rate and for mean-time-to-remediation
at corpus scale (E1, E3; Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\textbf{(C2)} Traceback reduced to one constant-time content-hash lookup over
the signed records, matched against two forensic baselines on identical
third-party inputs and stress-tested under post-ingestion drift (E2,
Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\textbf{(C3)} A reproducible open artifact (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) with a
digest-pinned real corpus, third-party attack and baseline code, and an
end-to-end harness.

\section{Related Work}

We position \sysname{} against five lines of work, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:related}. \textbf{Detection and query-time filtering.} GMTP
filters poisoned documents by masking high-impact tokens and checking their
probability under a masked language model, eliminating over 90\% of poisoned
content at retrieval~\cite{gmtp}; RevPRAG detects poisoned responses at inference
time from LLM activations~\cite{revprag}; RAGPart and RAGMask are retrieval-stage
defenses that partition or flag retrieved content~\cite{ragpartmask}. These
mitigate the \emph{effect} of an attack but keep no provenance, perform no
attribution, and offer no recovery; they are a complementary query-time layer,
and on detector accuracy a filter like GMTP is the stronger tool. \sysname{}
targets the operational axis those tools do not address and claims no novelty in
detection. RevPRAG and GMTP act at retrieval/inference time, not at forensic
time over a fixed suspect list, so they are not run as attributors here.

\textbf{Forensic attribution.} Poison Forensics established pipeline-level
traceback, tracing a successful poisoning attack back to the responsible training
samples by iterative clustering and unlearning-based pruning~\cite{poison-forensics};
the RAG-specific attributors build on this lineage. RAGForensics introduced
traceback for RAG, re-retrieving candidate texts and prompting an LLM to judge
which are poisoned~\cite{ragforensics}; RAGOrigin extends this to black-box
responsibility attribution with an adaptive scope and rank/relevance/influence
scoring~\cite{ragorigin}. All three report strong attribution accuracy, but all
are reactive, triggered per bad response, and their per-candidate scoring is
costly at forensic time; none attests provenance at ingestion or revokes a
source. \sysname{} is complementary: it pays a small one-time signing cost at
ingestion so that attribution later becomes a constant-time lookup, and adds the
missing revocation step. We run both RAG attributors as baselines on identical
suspects in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

\textbf{Ingestion-time attestation.} Generic supply-chain signing analogues sign
at file granularity, carry no detector verdicts, and are not instantiated for the
fragments that RAG retrieves: Sigstore/Cosign signs whole
artifacts,\footnote{\href{https://edu.chainguard.dev/open-source/sigstore/cosign/how-to-sign-an-sbom-with-cosign/}{How
to Sign an SBOM with Cosign, Chainguard Academy, 2024.}} as does the CycloneDX
ML-BOM. D-RAG admits content
with provenance and human expert verification on a blockchain, but at document
or source granularity, with heavy machinery and no one-command
purge.\footnote{\href{https://aircconline.com/csit/papers/vol15/csit150417.pdf}{D-RAG:
A Privacy-Preserving Framework for Decentralized RAG using Blockchain, CSIT,
2025.}} \sysname{} is orthogonal: per-chunk granularity, a real public-key
signature, and the recovery layer of traceback plus revocation that the analogy
implies, as the rightmost columns of Table~\ref{tab:related} make explicit.

\textbf{Cryptographic provenance and receipts.} A line of work signs
provenance into the RAG pipeline at varying granularities, which makes per-chunk
signed provenance itself unsurprising rather than the locus of novelty.
Concurrently, VectorPin signs each individual RAG embedding to its source-content
hash, producing model, and issuer key with an Ed25519 signature, giving
tamper-evidence against steganographic embedding-store exfiltration, but stops at
per-embedding integrity without traceback or revocation~\cite{VectorPin}.
Policy-governed RAG binds each material answer to cryptographically anchored
source evidence and emits portable COSE/JOSE compliance receipts for regulated
workflows~\cite{leray2025policy}; ZKPROV uses zero-knowledge proofs to verify
that responses were produced from authority-certified datasets without revealing
their contents, at dataset granularity~\cite{ZKPROV}; and, in the speech domain,
MerkleSpeech commits chunk-localised perceptual fingerprints in an issuer-signed
Merkle tree for public-key-verifiable attribution~\cite{MerkleSpeech}. These
establish signed or verifiable provenance, per embedding, per answer, per
dataset, or per audio chunk, yet none pairs it with constant-time content-hash
traceback and one-command source revocation over a text RAG corpus. \sysname{}
adopts per-chunk signed provenance as a means and contributes that recovery layer.

\textbf{Provenance defense-in-depth.} The closest prior work, RAGShield, also
treats corpus poisoning as a supply-chain problem: it attests documents at
ingestion (C2PA-inspired, Ed25519 by design but simulated with HMAC in its
prototype), weights retrieval by trust tier, and flags cross-source
contradictions, reporting 0.0\% attack success across a five-tier poisoning
benchmark over 500 Natural Questions passages~\cite{ragshield}; the same
evaluation reports a blind spot, an in-place replacement attack (its T6 tier)
that still reaches 17.5\% attack success. It signs at
document granularity rather than per chunk and provides neither signature-keyed
traceback nor source revocation, the gap Table~\ref{tab:related} makes explicit;
we compare capabilities rather than run it head to head because it releases no
artifact.

\section{Design}\label{sec:design}

\sysname{} is an ingestion gate (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). For each chunk it
computes a SHA-256 content hash, runs a best-effort detector suite, assembles a
canonical byte string over the provenance tuple (chunk id, source URI,
principal, content hash, detector verdicts, timestamp, signer identity,
granularity), signs it with Ed25519, and writes the signed record into the
datastore. The datastore maintains two side indices. A content-hash index maps a
chunk hash to its signed record, so an exact content-hash match resolves a
suspect chunk to the principal recorded for it at ingestion in constant time:
this exact-hash match is the traceback operation. A principal index maps a
principal to its set of chunk ids, so revoking a source enumerates exactly its
chunks without scanning the corpus. Because the signed message binds the
verdicts and the content hash, a record cannot be replayed for different content
or different admitting verdicts without invalidating the signature, and any
holder of the public key can verify provenance.

\textbf{Threat model and scope.} The attacker controls or compromises a web
source ingested by the corpus and injects poisoned texts, including the
split-view/frontrunning case where a source mutates after
collection~\cite{poisonedrag,carlini}. The protected asset is the integrity and
\emph{traceability} of the indexed corpus; the secondary asset is
mean-time-to-remediation after a source is found compromised. The operator is
trusted and holds the private key; ingestion is where provenance is sealed.
Detection at ingestion is best-effort and may have false negatives, so
\sysname{}'s value is forensic and operational; query-time
defenses~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask} are a complementary layer.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Behaviour is configured entirely by environment
variables (prefix \texttt{RAGTRAP\_}); nothing is hardcoded. Every run logs the
resolved configuration, each input with its content hash, every step, and all
outputs to a timestamped file. A manifest pins each third-party input by SHA-256
(Table~\ref{tab:data}) alongside model identities. The private key is generated
per run and never written to disk.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

We run four experiments on the datasets of Table~\ref{tab:data}, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:exps}. \textbf{E1} validates the instrument on a labelled corpus,
including exact-principal purging with no collateral. \textbf{E3} measures the
surgical revocation against document-level purging for false-purge rate and
mean-time-to-remediation, with per-chunk ingestion overhead at corpus scale (the
lead result, delivering C1). \textbf{E2} compares forensic-time attribution on
identical real suspects against two baselines (delivering C2), and measures
recall under post-ingestion drift. \textbf{E4} confirms the suspects are
genuinely dangerous via attack success. The attack, retrieval, ground-truth
labels, and baselines are all third-party artifacts that \sysname{} did not
author, so the suspect set used for attribution is independent of the mechanism
under test.

\textbf{E1: Instrument validation.} On a labelled corpus of \EZeroChunks{}
chunks, all signed records verify, a tampered message is rejected, and revocation
purges exactly the targeted principal's \EZeroPurged{} chunks with no collateral
removal. This confirms the gate, signature verification, and the revocation
index behave as specified before any comparison.

\textbf{E2: Forensic-time attribution on the real attack.} The clean substrate
is the BEIR~\cite{beir} Natural Questions corpus~\cite{naturalquestions}; the
attack is PoisonedRAG's released adversarial passages~\cite{poisonedrag}; the
suspects and labels come from the released RAGOrigin attack-feedback file (the
contexts surfaced by the \texttt{e5} dense retriever~\cite{e5} for each of
\Nquestions{} target questions, with third-party poisoned/clean labels). For
each question we ingest its retrieved contexts through the \sysname{} gate (real
Ed25519 per chunk). At forensic time the suspects are the top-\TopK{} retrieved
contexts per question (\NsuspectsE{} suspects: \NpoisonE{} poisoned, \NcleanE{}
clean), exactly what an attacked pipeline feeds the LLM. Three attributors run on
the \emph{identical} \NsuspectsE{} suspects, all executed here: \sysname{}'s
constant-time content-hash lookup; the published RAGForensics LLM-judge loop run
verbatim over a local \BLjudge{} model (one model call per context); and the
published RAGOrigin responsibility scoring run over a local \ROproxy{} proxy
(answer-loss, question-loss, and retrieval score, z-normalized and
K-means-thresholded, two proxy calls per context).

detection score and is omitted from the accuracy rows; the accuracy of interest
is how recall degrades once provenance is incomplete, which we measure next.

\textbf{E2-drift: recall under post-ingestion mutation.} \sysname{} resolves a
suspect only if its bytes were admitted through the gate, so a poisoned chunk
whose retrieved bytes drift after ingestion (a paraphrase, the split-view
case~\cite{carlini}) will not hash-match. Paraphrasing a fraction $p$ of the
poisoned suspects after ingestion drops recall from \RTrecall{} at $p{=}0$ to
\textbf{\RTrecallDriftThree{}} (95\%~CI
[\RTrecallDriftThreeLo{},\,\RTrecallDriftThreeHi{}]) at $p{=}0.3$ and
\textbf{\RTrecallDriftFive{}} (95\%~CI
[\RTrecallDriftFiveLo{},\,\RTrecallDriftFiveHi{}]) at $p{=}0.5$
(Figure~\ref{fig:drift}). Precision stays at \RTprecision{} because a hash match
is exact; the missed chunks are false negatives. This is exactly the regime a
competent, content-mutating adversary operates in~\cite{carlini}: recovering
drifted variants requires near-duplicate or semantic matching, for instance
MinHash~\cite{broder1997minhash}, SimHash~\cite{charikar2002simhash}, or
locality-sensitive hashing~\cite{indyk1998lsh}, and is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\textbf{E3: Surgical revocation, then cost at scale.} The lead result is that
per-chunk revocation purges a compromised source without collateral. On
\EThreeDocs{} real partially-poisoned documents (real NQ passages with real
PoisonedRAG passages injected under one principal), purging at document
granularity removes whole documents and so over-purges their benign fragments at
a false-purge rate of \textbf{\PerDocFP{}} (95\%~CI
[\PerDocFPLo{},\,\PerDocFPHi{}], \PerDocFalse{} of \PerDocPurged{} removed chunks
were clean), whereas \sysname{}'s per-chunk principal index localises and removes
exactly the poisoned chunks at a false-purge rate of \textbf{\PerChunkFP{}}. This
clean excision, not the speed, is what the recovery layer buys.

The recovery operations are also cheap. Treating one PoisonedRAG attacker
principal as compromised, we remove it two ways on the real corpus: \sysname{}'s
\texttt{revoke-source} batch purge versus a manual loop that scans the whole
corpus. The fair comparison is maintaining a provenance index versus not: with an
index, revocation is an $O(1)$ lookup of the principal's \RevChunks{} chunk ids
followed by their deletion, \FullRevMttr~$\mu$s on the full \FullChunks{}-chunk
corpus, while without one the scan over every chunk takes \FullManMttr~ms; the
\FullMttrRatio$\times$ ratio simply reflects $O(1)$ versus $O(\text{corpus})$ work
and grows with corpus size, from \SmallMttrRatio$\times$ at \SmallPassages{}
passages to \FullMttrRatio$\times$ at the full corpus (Figure~\ref{fig:revoke}):
the surgical purge stays flat while the manual scan tracks the corpus, so the
advantage widens by orders of magnitude as the corpus grows.
The cost of the index is paid once at ingestion: per-chunk Ed25519 signing takes
about \EdLatency~$\mu$s ($\sim$\EdThroughput{} chunks/s, single-threaded),
producing a fixed \EdSig{}-byte signature in an \EdRecord{}-byte record, about
\EdOverHmac$\times$ the cost of a symmetric HMAC stand-in (\HmacLatency~$\mu$s,
\HmacSig{}-byte tag) in exchange for non-repudiable provenance verifiable without
the secret key.

\textbf{E4: Attack-success context.} To confirm the suspects are genuinely
dangerous, we feed the top-\AsrTopK{} retrieved contexts to a local \AsrGen{}
generation model and check the answer: the attack steers it to the attacker's
target answer in \textbf{\AsrPoint\%} of the \AsrN{} questions (95\%~CI
[\AsrLo{},\,\AsrHi{}]). This confirms that the chunks \sysname{} attributes do
mislead the pipeline it protects.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Scope: what \sysname{} is and is not.} \sysname{} is a forensic and
operational layer for recovery and for inventory-and-exclusion: given that a
source has been flagged compromised, it attributes the source's chunks and
removes them cleanly. It does not detect or prevent poisoning, and it relies on
an external detector or a human analyst to flag the compromised source in the
first place; the ingestion detectors are best-effort and out of scope, and
query-time defenses~\cite{gmtp,ragpartmask} remain a complementary layer.
Attribution rests on an exact content-hash match, so it is not robust to a
content-mutating adversary: any post-ingestion paraphrase or split-view
mutation~\cite{carlini} of a chunk's bytes evades the hash, dropping recall to
\RTrecallDriftThree{} at 30\% drift and \RTrecallDriftFive{} at 50\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:drift}). Closing this gap needs near-duplicate or semantic
matching~\cite{broder1997minhash,charikar2002simhash,indyk1998lsh} and is the
central piece of future work. Per-chunk signed provenance, the substrate the
recovery layer is built on, is not claimed as novel: prior and concurrent systems
already sign provenance per embedding, per answer, per dataset, or per audio
chunk~\cite{VectorPin,leray2025policy,ZKPROV,MerkleSpeech}.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} E2's suspects and labels come from one released
attack-feedback set over NQ; the corpus-scale results (E3) use the full
\FullPassages{}-passage corpus, and the structural arguments (constant work for
lookup and revocation) do not depend on scale. The two baselines are the
published RAGForensics and RAGOrigin procedures run over local open models rather
than the hosted models the original papers used; we report our own measured
recall, precision, latency, and model-call count on identical inputs. Adaptive
and multi-attacker regimes, and other corpora (HotpotQA, MS-MARCO, whose released
attack data we also obtained), are future work. We assume a trusted operator
holding the private key; key compromise is a residual risk.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Corpus poisoning is cheap and effective, standards demand
provenance and quarantine, and the closest tools either filter at query time or
attribute post hoc with no recovery step. The operational question we opened,
once a source is found compromised, how precisely and how cheaply an operator can
purge it, has a concrete answer: by sealing per-chunk signed provenance at
ingestion, \sysname{} revokes a compromised source surgically, removing exactly
its chunks at a false-purge rate of \textbf{\PerChunkFP{}} where document-level
purging discards \PerDocFP{} of what it removes, and turns traceback into a
content-hash lookup that costs no model calls. Both recovery operations are cheap
because the index does the work, but the value is the clean excision, not the
speed. The limit is equally concrete: the exact-hash basis means \sysname{} is
not robust to a content-mutating adversary, recall falling to
\RTrecallDriftThree{} under 30\% post-ingestion drift, and it presupposes an
external detector or analyst to flag the source. Pairing this recovery layer with
near-duplicate matching is the natural next step. We release \sysname{} as a
reproducible, double-blind
artifact.\footnote{\href{https://anonymous.4open.science}{Anonymized artifact
repository (link provided for double-blind review).}}
